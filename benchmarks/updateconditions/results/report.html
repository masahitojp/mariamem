## What this run can say

Internal wait/notify tracing and the query watchdog are absent, yet the long tail did not recur. This does not establish that tracing had no effect; it does not support attributing non-reproduction solely to the former collector either. The new two-write workload also differs from the old single-write suite.

The second UPDATE was faster within 124/144 immediate children and 131/144 delayed children. All six process medians also have second<first. A first-versus-second difference survives a500ms pause before the first write; elapsed time alone did not remove that ordinary difference. Cache/undo/data state and query order remain confounded.

All three paired process comparisons have a higher first-UPDATE median in the delayed condition; the second-UPDATE median is also higher in all three. These query timings exclude the deliberate pause. Waiting500ms is therefore not supported as a simple way to make ordinary UPDATE faster. The smaller observed maxima in the delayed arm are retained, but zero>100ms in both arms cannot establish rare-tail suppression.

These ordinary sub-ms/few-ms effects do not explain the prior355ms wait. Stop without additional sampling.
