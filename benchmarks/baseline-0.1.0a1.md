# 0.1.0a1 manual baseline — 2026-09-23

User-provided manual observations, retained without rerunning or recalculating
the benchmarks. These are not results generated by the scripts in this directory.
Full raw samples, hardware details, warmup/cache policy, sampling interval, and
the exact original commands are unavailable in this record. Do not combine
different measurement sessions into one precise performance claim.

Known context: macOS development machine; Python 3.9.6; mariamem 0.1.0a1;
PyMySQL 1.2.3; mariamem reported `13.1.0-MariaDB-embedded`; Testcontainers used
`mariadb:12.3`. mariamem used separate Go/Wasmer processes and precompiled `.wasmu`.

## Fresh ready-to-query (10 runs, seconds)

Start fresh DB, connect using PyMySQL, `SELECT 1`.

| Backend | Min | Median | Mean | Max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mariamem | 0.545 | 0.720 | 1.000 | 1.717 |
| Testcontainers | 4.262 | 5.345 | 5.295 | 7.843 |

This measures lifecycle latency, not MariaDB SQL execution speed. The observed
median difference is not a universal multiplier or a structural lower bound.

## Seeded independent DBs (1,000 rows, InnoDB, 10 DBs)

Testcontainers starts and seeds a fresh container each time. mariamem prepares
one template/snapshot and restores ten independent forks.

| Backend | Min | Median | Mean | Max | Sum of DB ready times |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mariamem forks | 0.575 | 0.833 | 0.857 | 1.323 | 8.566 s |
| Testcontainers | 3.312 | 5.307 | 4.872 | 5.410 | 48.724 s |

mariamem preparation + forks: **9.525 s**, or **0.953 s per isolated DB including
preparation**. Preparation cost must not be omitted from amortized comparisons.

Eight-DB mariamem peak was observed around 1.13 s. No large hidden startup spike
was observed in that session; the sampling resolution does not rule one out.
The nonlinear RSS behavior remains unexplained. Memory pressure, shared pages,
and runtime decommit are hypotheses, not established causes.

## Interpretation limits

In this measured environment mariamem had lower lifecycle latency than fresh
Testcontainers. This does not imply faster SQL semantics, universal superiority,
or replacement of tests requiring the production OS/container environment.
The current architecture is multi-process and already uses precompiled artifacts.
These observations do not promise sub-100 ms startup or justify performance
changes without separate profiling.
